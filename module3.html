Choose Your Direction

Define your audience, their problem, and your positioning statement.

⏱ ~20 minutes
📐 Direction Compass Worksheet
🔒 Lock-In: Your positioning statement
Choose who you help

Choose a specific audience and a problem your skill stack can solve. Use this focus to evaluate projects and explain your work.

You can revise this direction as you learn from clients. Start with one audience and one problem.

Name the problem

Describe the situation that makes someone seek help. Include its cost, how often it happens, and what happens if it continues.

Example
A service founder loses leads because inquiries receive late replies. An inquiry and follow-up process addresses that problem.

How expensive is this problem?
Estimate the cost in lost revenue, time, or delayed work. Note how you calculated it.
How often does it recur?
One-time problems justify one-time projects. Recurring problems justify recurring engagements and retainers.
What happens if it remains unsolved?
Describe the effect on their time, costs, or results.

ICP Builder — Document 02
Ideal Client Profile

Describe the client’s situation, needs, and buying decision. This becomes your Ideal Client Profile.

Use what you know from conversations or research. Mark assumptions you still need to check.
Who they are (demographics)
Age range, professional background, how long they've been in business, revenue stage, team size.
What they want (goals)
What does success look like to them in 12 months? What are they working toward?
What they struggle with (pain points)
The specific frustrations they experience regularly. Be concrete — not 'they're stressed' but what specifically stresses them.
What they've already tried
Solutions they've attempted that didn't fully work. This tells you what NOT to sound like.
Where they live online
Which platforms, communities, podcasts, newsletters. Where do they look for answers?
What keeps them up at night
Describe their main concern about this problem.
What they say vs. what they mean
"I need help with my brand" often means something else entirely. What's the surface ask vs. the real need?
What makes them ready to invest
What change makes solving this problem a priority?
